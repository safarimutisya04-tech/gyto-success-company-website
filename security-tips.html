Get Security Tips

The following notes are intended as a brief reminder of actions to be taken or things to remember to enhance your personal security. They are not exhaustive and are not intended to cause undue alarm � but rather to provide reassurance that you, your family, friends, and colleagues know how to react in a security situation.

Travel & Road Safety

  • Take time to plan activities. Know the exact route before travelling and leave a planned itinerary with a responsible person.
  • Carry a list of emergency names and phone numbers � both in the car and saved in your phone.
  • Use common-sense security measures and become familiar with your surroundings. Trust your instincts during travel.
  • Travelling in convoy with two or more vehicles is often the safest approach in areas of conflict or high crime.
  • Observe obstructions or checkpoints from a distance without stopping first � assess whether the situation looks normal before proceeding.
  • Keep valuables out of sight. It is best to travel without valuables where possible.
  • Keep all car doors locked and windows more than halfway up at all times.
  • Vary your routes and times of travel to avoid predictable patterns.
  • Avoid driving late at night, especially in unfamiliar areas.
  • Do not travel alone, especially if you are a woman or travelling through a high-risk area.

Vehicle Security

  • Think before parking. Avoid isolated or unpopulated areas. Choose well-lit, highly visible parking spaces whenever possible.
  • Never leave valuables in plain sight. If you must leave anything of value in the car, ensure it is completely out of sight.
  • Always lock your vehicle. Check that all doors are secured and no windows are left open � even briefly.
  • Never leave your keys in the car. Thieves will check obvious hiding spots like under the mat or above the sun visor.
  • Fit locking wheel nuts on all wheels where possible. These may not stop a determined thief but will deter most.
  • Use a steering lock as an additional visual deterrent, particularly for motorcycles and older vehicles.

Personal Safety

  • Good planning combined with strong situational awareness are your best protection. Avoid putting yourself in vulnerable situations.
  • When walking on the road, walk on the side facing oncoming traffic so you can see approaching vehicles.
  • Consider carrying a personal alarm, particularly in urban or suburban areas. Both gas-operated and battery-operated types are available.
  • Keep your mobile phone fully charged before leaving the house � it is one of your most important safety tools.
  • When in a car, ensure windows are up and all doors are locked. Check you have adequate fuel before setting off.
  • Keep a mobile phone charger in the car in case of emergencies, but ensure the phone itself is already charged.

Carjacking Prevention

Carjacking can occur when stopped at a traffic light, when parked, at a checkpoint, or while driving. The following factors increase your risk:

  • Driving an expensive or conspicuous vehicle through high-risk areas.
  • Travelling through unknown areas late at night or alone.
  • Conspicuously displaying items of value such as phones, laptops, or jewellery.
  • Following predictable driving routines at the same times each day.
  • Not paying attention to what is happening around you or on the roadway.
  • Stay alert at all times, particularly at traffic lights, roundabouts, and road junctions where vehicles slow down.

Behaviour at Security Checkpoints

  • Think and plan ahead before approaching a checkpoint area.
  • Never drink and drive � impaired judgement significantly increases risk.
  • Drive slowly on approach and reduce speed well before reaching the checkpoint.
  • At night, dim your headlights and switch on your interior light so officers can see you clearly.
  • Keep your hands visible at all times and remain calm and friendly throughout the interaction.
  • Show your ID when requested. Answer all questions honestly and cooperate with any vehicle search � do not resist.

Laptop & Device Security

  • Never store access codes, passwords, or PINs in your laptop bag. Without your password, a thief has limited ability to access your data.
  • Avoid using branded computer bags that advertise you are carrying a laptop. Use a plain padded briefcase or bag instead.
  • Always carry your laptop as hand luggage when travelling by air � never check it in with your hold luggage.
  • When travelling by car, lock your laptop in the boot and out of sight when not in use.
  • Enable full-disk encryption and remote-wipe capabilities on all your devices.
  • Use strong, unique passwords and enable two-factor authentication on all important accounts.
